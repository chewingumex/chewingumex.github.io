// Overview

An end-to-end Graph Retrieval-Augmented Generation (Graph RAG) system built on a corpus of 2,147 BBC News articles. Every article is processed by a two-pass Gemma4 LLM pipeline that extracts named entities and their relationships, which are then wired into a NetworkX knowledge graph. Articles are linked to one another through cosine similarity (SIMILAR_TO edges at a 0.85 threshold), creating a semantic bridge across the corpus.

The graph is then clustered using a two-level Leiden algorithm — 74 broad communities (Level 0) subdivided into 577 sub-communities (Level 1) — and each cluster is summarised into a community report by the LLM. At query time, an LLM-based router classifies the intent as local (specific entity), global (thematic), or both, and assembles the right mix of article evidence and community reports as context for the final answer.

// Architecture

Six-step pipeline

01
Extract
2-pass Gemma4 LLM: extract then review & correct. 5 embedded fields per article. 2,147 JSON files.
›
02
Clean
6 deterministic passes — normalise, remap 100+ raw types to 7 canonical, deduplicate, referential integrity.
›
03
Build Graph
NetworkX DiGraph. MENTIONS + semantic SIMILAR_TO edges (cosine ≥ 0.85). 13,406 nodes · 32,496 edges.
›
04
Cluster
Two-pass Leiden: L0 on full graph (74 communities), L1 on each L0 subgraph (577 sub-communities).
›
05
Summarise
Two-phase LLM run: all text generation first (Gemma4 resident), then batch embeddings (embeddinggemma). 651 reports.
›
06
Retrieve & Answer
LLM router → cosine search → graph hop → context assembly → Gemma4 answer generation.

// Engineering Choices

Four decisions that mattered

Graph Structure
Two-pass Leiden over hierarchical_leiden
The standard hierarchical_leiden only sub-divides communities that exceed a size threshold. On this corpus, 85% of documents had no Level-1 assignment, making fine-grain retrieval impossible. Replacing it with two independent Leiden passes — one on the full graph, one on each Level-0 induced subgraph — gives genuine finer-grain coverage at both levels.
LLM Efficiency
Two-phase summarisation to eliminate model-swap overhead
The original per-community approach alternated between the chat model (for text generation) and the embedding model (for vectorisation) on every community — ~1,300 model-swap cycles that dominated runtime. Separating into Phase 1 (all generation, chat model stays loaded) and Phase 2 (all embeddings in batches of 32) cut this to 2 model loads total.
Query Routing
LLM classifier replaces brittle token heuristics
A simple entity-name presence check would misclassify thematic queries that happen to mention known entities. The router instead sends the query plus up to 5 matched entity names (ranked by length so "Tony Blair" outscores "BBC") to a few-shot LLM classifier at temperature 0, returning one of local · global · both. Heuristic is retained as a fallback if the LLM call fails.
Retrieval Quality
Level-aware global search prevents L1 from always winning
If global search runs over all 651 community reports, Level-1 fine-grain reports systematically outscore Level-0 broad reports in cosine similarity (they are more specific, closer to any given query token). For global queries, search is restricted to L0 reports only to retrieve genuine thematic synthesis. For both queries, all levels are available.

// Query Demo

Three retrieval paths in action

Illustrative outputs showing routing logic, context assembly, and answer generation across the three paths.

"What did Tony Blair do regarding Iraq?"
local tony blair · iraq · david kelly
5 articles · 48 entity triples · 3 similar articles via SIMILAR_TO graph hop
Tony Blair was central to the UK's involvement in Iraq, having committed British forces to the US-led coalition in March 2003. Coverage in the graph centres on three threads: (1) the September 2002 dossier and the contested "45-minute claim" — that Iraq could deploy weapons of mass destruction within 45 minutes — which Blair cited in Parliament as justification for military action; (2) the death of government weapons expert Dr David Kelly, who had spoken to BBC journalist Andrew Gilligan, and the subsequent Hutton Inquiry; and (3) the political aftermath, in which the Hutton Report largely exonerated the government and heavily criticised the BBC's editorial standards, triggering the resignations of Director General Greg Dyke and Chairman Gavyn Davies. Across the retrieved articles, Blair consistently defended the case for military intervention while facing growing parliamentary opposition and public scepticism.
Path: local · context assembled from 5 articles + 48 triples · graph-hop via MENTIONS and SIMILAR_TO edges
"What are the main themes in sports coverage?"
global no specific entity matched → thematic route
L0 community reports 8, 12, 19 · level_filter = 0 (broad summaries only)
Sports coverage in the corpus is dominated by football — particularly Premier League club developments, the England national team, and the transfer market. Community report L0-8 identifies ownership and finance as a recurring sub-theme: Roman Abramovich's acquisition of Chelsea and the resulting spending shift restructured competitive expectations across the top flight. International football (World Cup qualifiers, Euro 2004) and individual player stories around Wayne Rooney, David Beckham's Real Madrid tenure, and Michael Owen feature prominently. Beyond football, community L0-12 covers cricket (England Test tours, the county season), rugby (Six Nations, Lions), and athletics. A cross-cutting theme in L0-19 is the commercialisation of sport: broadcasting rights disputes, doping controversies, and the blurring of sports governance with business interests.
Path: global · community reports searched at Level 0 only (74 broad reports) · level_filter=0 prevents fine-grain L1 reports from dominating
"What is the relationship between the BBC and the government?"
both bbc · government · hutton inquiry
5 articles + 48 triples (local) · 3 community reports across L0 & L1 (global) · level_filter = None
The relationship in this period was defined by the fallout from BBC political journalist Andrew Gilligan's May 2003 Today programme report — that Downing Street had "sexed up" the September 2002 weapons dossier to make the case for war in Iraq appear stronger than the intelligence supported. The government denied the claim and demanded a retraction; the BBC's Board of Governors refused. The subsequent death of government scientist Dr David Kelly — identified as Gilligan's source — triggered the Hutton Inquiry, which reported in January 2004 and broadly cleared the government while criticising the BBC's editorial controls. Director General Greg Dyke and Chairman Gavyn Davies resigned. At the thematic level, community reports show this episode sits within broader recurring tensions: debates over the BBC licence fee, accusations of political bias from both Conservative and Labour benches, and the question of editorial independence from government in the context of a publicly-funded broadcaster.
Path: both · local path retrieves specific article evidence + entity triples · global path adds thematic community synthesis across all levels

A follow-up experiment trains a Graph Masked Autoencoder on the document–entity bipartite graph, compressing each article's 768-dim sentence embedding into a 256-dim vector that encodes not just semantic content but entity co-occurrence and community structure. Incoming queries are projected into that space via a linear map fit on the training documents.
