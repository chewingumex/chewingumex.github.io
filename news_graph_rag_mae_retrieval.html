// Retrieval System Comparison · BBC News Graph RAG

Baseline vs
MAE-Enhanced Retrieval

The baseline system retrieves documents using 768-dim embeddings from a pre-trained sentence encoder (embeddinggemma). The MAE-enhanced system fuses two views per document — summary and relationship triples — via a learned attention mechanism, then trains a graph masked autoencoder on the document–entity bipartite graph to produce 256-dim embeddings that encode semantic content, relational structure, and entity co-occurrence. Queries are projected into the MAE space via a linear map fit on the training documents.

// Caveats & Limitations

What to bear in mind

Both systems are research prototypes. The improvements shown are real but should be interpreted in context. The following limitations apply.

Both systems

  • Dense retrieval only No BM25 or keyword fallback. Rare terms ("cricket", niche proper nouns) fail silently — the system returns plausible-sounding but wrong articles with no indication of low confidence. A sparse+dense hybrid is the standard fix.
  • No labelled evaluation data All metrics are intrinsic proxies (community coherence, entity overlap). No human relevance judgements exist for this corpus. Real-world retrieval quality may differ from what the benchmarks suggest.
  • Small, time-bounded corpus 2,147 BBC articles from a specific period. Community structure and entity clusters are artefacts of that slice of news. Neither system generalises to other corpora without retraining.
  • LLM-generated community reports Global search answers draw on Leiden community summaries written by gemma4. These may contain hallucinations or simplifications that propagate into final answers without any traceability.
  • No entity resolution "SEC" and "US Securities and Exchange Commission" are separate graph nodes. Partial name variants and abbreviations fragment entity clusters and reduce both retrieval precision and community coherence.

MAE-specific

  • Linear query projection is an approximation The MAE learns a non-linear transformation of the document space. Mapping incoming queries into that space via a linear least-squares fit is a pragmatic shortcut. A learned query encoder trained end-to-end would be more accurate, but requires query–document supervision pairs.
  • Rare topics may perform worse than baseline The MAE receives almost no gradient signal from underrepresented topics. Entity connections pull rare documents toward the dominant cluster, potentially erasing the very signal the baseline preserves. The cricket failure in this report is the clearest example.

Natural next steps: sparse+dense hybrid retrieval (BM25 + MAE), a learned query encoder trained on synthetic Q&A pairs, entity resolution via gazetteer or LLM normalisation, and a proper held-out evaluation set with human relevance labels.
